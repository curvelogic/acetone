At scale, and in conformance.

Phase 9 is where acetone stops being demonstrably correct on small inputs and starts being measurably correct at size. Two halves had to land together: the query engine had to stop declining large parts of openCypher, and the storage and verification paths had to stop assuming everything fits in memory or in one pass.

The uncomfortable slide

What the pass rate doesn't tell you.

The conformance statement went through three review rounds. Every published number was right from the first commit; every finding was in the prose interpreting them. Three claims I wrote were false:

  • "Passes execute the query." 274 of 2185 (12.5%) don't — they're credited on a front-end rejection where the TCK expected a compile error. 27 of those verify only that some parse error occurred, not which.
  • "No limitation can inflate the count." Three can: the unverified parse-rejection reason; identity-less entity comparison (a structurally identical wrong node passes); and a write-query token escape that files real gaps as deferrals.
  • "The biggest gap is subquery and CSV syntax." CALL {}, FOREACH and LOAD CSV appear in zero corpus files. The real answer is temporal types — 1025 scenarios, 90% of that bucket.

All three are now in the published statement, under a heading that says which mechanisms can turn a defect into a pass.

WHAT THE LAB COULD NOT SHOW

All those rows were measured against the in-memory source — Session, the shipped path, had no range or key seek at all. Wiring them (PRs #219, #221) exposed the thing the in-memory source never pays for: a seek does one random point read per row; the scan reads sequentially. Past ~2.5% selectivity the "optimisation" was up to 37× slower than no index.

Fixed by declining, not optimising: past 1024 candidates the source returns "cannot serve — scan instead", before paying for point reads. 51 rows: 8.2 ms vs 105.4 ms. 24,973 rows: 111.9 ms, where the naive seek took 2570.2 ms. No regime is slower than the scan.

And both of the lab's populated cases fail on the shipped path. The range case (~16,000 rows, 16× the cap) declines and scans: 1.0×, not 13.8×. The composite case, reproduced at the lab's own 2.9% bucket ratio, fires and loses: 324.9 ms indexed vs 88.2 ms unindexed (1.5× slower even packed) where the lab says 27.6× faster. A seek does a random point read per row; the lab's in-memory source pays nothing for that. Both cases are genuinely scan-shaped — no cost model would change it.

What does work on the shipped path: selective ranges (2.8×), absence proofs on an empty bucket (2.6–3.3×), and point lookups. Yours to rule on: is that enough, or should the lab cases be re-parameterised and re-run?

Still open, and not ours: the equality seek path has the same cliff and no cap — 1248 ms indexed vs 67 ms unindexed on main today — from PR #123, not this phase. Measured and recorded on acetone-2ck.2.

The gate earned its keep

Four things that would have shipped.

#217Four rounds, and real data loss. My first anchor-completeness rule failed every legacy repo forever with unworkable advice. My second — "safe if any tree anchors it" — the reviewer drove to an actual missing chunk: fsck clean → acetone gc → git gc → gone, because the covering anchor was one gc itself deletes. My third made the claim false again on the commonest shape. ADR-0063 exists because of that sequence.
#218Three rounds on honesty. All 14 published figures correct; every finding in the prose. See slide 4.
#206Silent row drops. Cross-type key pins dropped rows, and the hints were dead on the main path.
#208A UNIQUE breach admitted. CBOR claim keys let -0.0 and 0.0 collide and falsely rejected NaN — fixed by using the memcomparable key encoding.

The common thread: every one was found by a reviewer executing the untested path, not by reading the diff.

8

Milestone security review · 19 PRs, 48 files, ~7000 insertions

Two blockers. Both closed.

BLOCKER 1 · PARSER

268 bytes → 53.8 GB

Chained comparisons cloned interior operands, so nesting doubled the AST per level — 67 s and 53.8 GB inside parse(), before any governor exists, and equally from any library consumer.

Fixed: the same bomb errors in 0.03 s at 5.6 MB.

BLOCKER 2 · EXECUTOR

One line, hangs forever

A pattern comprehension with a fresh anchor is evaluated per row and re-scanned the whole node map at zero governor charge.

Fixed: anchor candidates charged uniformly against a dedicated budget (ADR-0064). Two earlier designs were tried and rejected in review — work-only (too loose) and borrowing the expansion budget (rejected ordinary joins).

Nine lesser findings filed as beads (six from the milestone review, two from PR #219's own review, one co-tenancy note). Defences that held: prune_loose's safety floor survived every gc/worktree scenario; ADR-0044 anchors survived a real git gc --prune=now --aggressive; ref-name injection failed closed; 3M mutated queries, no panics; and no dependency added, bumped or re-featured all phase.

Decisions taken

Three ADRs for the agenda.

ADR-0062

Streaming import

Pull-based extraction, batched staging, statement/stored UNIQUE layers, workspace reset on failure. Includes the honest memory scoping rather than a flat "bounded memory" claim.

ADR-0063

Workspace anchor coverage

Self-anchoring mandatory for every live version; borrowed coverage for exactly one shape — a superseded pre-ADR-0014 ref no writer can upgrade — and only from durable, gc-enumerable sources. Written because the first rule was unsound.

ADR-0064

Scanning gets its own budget

max_scanned_candidates, not the expansion budget — borrowing that one rejected ordinary nested-loop joins. Adds a public field to QueryLimits and a variant to ResourceLimit, both in the frozen surface, inside a 0.3.x "additive only" series — and the CI freeze gate cannot see either, since public-api.txt records only the re-export line. Needs your ruling.

PROCESS · DISCLOSED DEVIATION

Fable 5 capacity was exhausted partway through. PR #217 rounds 2–4, PR #218, the security review and PR #219 ran at Opus — the strongest tier the session could reach — rather than downgrading silently or skipping the gate. Recorded on the beads as well as in the report. Compliant with ADR-0009's definition, but a change from previous phases.

For the boundary review

What's yours to rule on.

  • Gate acetone-2ck.1. All four criteria have evidence; both security blockers are closed. Yours to close — agents never close it.
  • Criterion 3. Both seek kinds now reach the product and beat a scan on selective inputs — but on the lab's own two populated cases, one declines to a scan and the other is slower than no index. Rule that reachability plus selective-case wins is enough, or re-parameterise the lab and re-run?
  • The equality-seek cliff on main. Arrived with PR #123, not this phase, but now measured: declaring an index makes an unselective equality query 18× slower. PR #221 demonstrates the remedy for the range path; acetone-2ck.2 carries both.
  • The API-freeze question ADR-0064 raises. A public field and enum variant added to types STABILITY.md freezes, in a patch series whose policy is additive-only, and invisible to the freeze gate. #[non_exhaustive] on both, or a minor bump?
  • The Opus tier substitution. Disclosed, compliant with ADR-0009 as written — but worth your view on whether the policy should say something explicit about capacity exhaustion.
  • Follow-ups crossing the boundary, each with its reason in the report: 2ck.12, 2ck.2, s1j, hi8, 1qj, taf and the security beads — all re-homed to 7qw. The epic still carries 16 pre-existing beads from the Phase 8 triage; whether those move is yours.
